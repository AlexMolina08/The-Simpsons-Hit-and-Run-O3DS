# World streaming investigation

## Target and baseline

The reported failure is a nearby road or section of scenery remaining invisible,
exposing the background, then appearing suddenly. The owner reports this generally
in Levels 2 and 3. Texture loading, geometry residency, culling and GPU submission
remain separate hypotheses until an incident trace identifies the failing stage.

The gameplay baseline is `61d1c78`. Parent `15ca26b` adds local builder
preservation of that runtime. Local commit `97e3a14` lends the loader mutex during
existing frame-pacing sleep. It is a partial improvement: subsequent ordinary
gameplay still reproduced missing scenery. The accepted opaque-world GPU submission policy,
visual features, prepared assets, normal draw distances and save format remain
the reference. The partial streaming fixes listed below are included in v1.2;
complete streaming, pop-in and population acceptance remains outstanding.
Nothing has been pushed, published or installed as a CIA during this investigation.

## Included in v1.2

- Lend the existing loader mutex during existing frame-pacing sleep, with the
  existing GPU upload guard. Busy frames still have little or no sleep to lend.
- Prioritize new exterior world demand over queued background model requests on
  Old 3DS. Preserve the active request address, earlier world-file order and the
  relative order of background requests and callbacks. Keep initial loading and
  other platforms on their existing policy.
- Preserve completed handler/callback values before invoking a reentrant callback
  that can reuse the freed request slot.
- Package the byte-verified road-first permutations of `l2z2.p3d` and `l2r2.p3d`.
  Native CIA packaging and browser v1.2 preparation both apply the same checked
  transform. Other maps retain their original chunk order. No chunk body, image,
  geometry, shader, save format or rendering-distance change is introduced.
- Use bounded existing query scratch for pedestrian path lookup when the accepted
  `QUERY_SCRATCH` option is enabled. Population limits and spawning policy remain
  unchanged; no higher visible population is claimed.

The normal browser-builder runtime now comes from `out/old3ds/`, generated by
`tools/3ds/build_old3ds.sh`, with checked release flags and provenance. Builder
preparation rejects older provenance without streaming policy version 1.
`build_cia.py` expects fully prepared v1.2 artwork and applies changes only to its
temporary staging copy. Atomic replacement protects hard-linked input originals.

The failed raw-reader, unaccepted prefetch/retention and pedestrian selection
experiments are excluded from the release branch. Their full source is preserved
on local branch `v12-streaming-investigation-snapshot` at `3475169b`, alongside
the private raw evidence under `out/local-dev/world-streaming-20261029/`.

These changes reduce a demonstrated floor gap. They do not eliminate late
surrounding scenery, establish a stable 30 FPS, or prove all-seven-map acceptance.
No further hardware runs were requested for this handoff.

## Remaining failure after the first fix

The owner marked another ordinary-play incident in `passive-fixed-play-1`.
The display capture shows absent scenery on the left of the northbound street,
exposing the background. The foreground road near the car being present does
not make this capture a pass. The owner also reports repeated gaps at speed,
visible world construction and slower frame rate.

The valid recorder retained 459 frames from the last 20 gameplay seconds, with
1,536 bound probes, no recording loss and a maximum speed of 32.829 m/s.
At the marker the player was at X/Z `(-237.722, 128.115)`.
`l2z2.p3d` took approximately 11.274 seconds from dispatch to completion.
The next road file, `l2r2.p3d`, waited approximately 3.559 seconds between enqueue
and dispatch and was still loading at the marker. Fresh ground intersections
near the car continued to hit; they do not establish surrounding visible scenery.

The mean instrumented frame work was 42.695 ms, versus 43.556 ms frame period.
The slow frame rate cannot be dismissed as recorder work outside that interval.
Sampled free normal memory stayed above 13,413,128 bytes and linear memory above
2,811,776 bytes. These samples do not establish every transient allocation peak.

A repeat focus sweep now follows the recorded northbound turn, using the recorded
camera field of view. A separate, explicitly modeled 12 ms main-thread reservation
stresses loader scheduling because clean focus sweeps omit ordinary driving work.
These sweeps are diagnostic experiments, never gameplay performance acceptance.
The reference queue audit shows eight pedestrian model requests and their callback
barriers ahead of the road file. A private candidate promotes pending exterior
world requests while retaining the active request identity, authored world-file
order and the relative order of background requests and callback barriers.
It does not preempt an active file. It remains disabled in normal release code
pending physical comparison.

Native texture bundle seeks also appear among the longest worker intervals.
The existing unbuffered-I/O diagnostic switch permits a separate same-byte asset
comparison. A seek sample is elapsed time while owning the loader mutex, not pure
CPU time. Neither queue priority nor an I/O hypothesis is yet a validated solution.

## Physical reproduction and scheduling evidence

The owner reproduced the transparent-world failure during ordinary Level 2
driving. Its loading events identify a westward approach through
`(111.78, -1.76)`, `(63.51, -5.19)`, `(-75.89, -18.27)` and
`(-136.80, -4.14)` in world X/Z coordinates. A first road-graph sweep took a
detour and did not visibly reproduce the failure. Replaying the recorded
approach with a forward camera did: the owner confirmed the failure, and a
repeat captured the nearby road ending in the background while scenery remained
partially absent. These physical screenshots are retained with
`l2-incident-control-2` under the investigation output directory.

The private focus sweep moves the streaming position and camera together at
32 m/s. It follows ordinary zone triggers and asynchronous loading without
waiting for resources at a waypoint. It holds at the endpoint to observe later
availability. The parked car does not follow this diagnostic focus, so a sweep
is not a gameplay physics or performance test. Real driving remains an
acceptance requirement. The general road-graph sweep separately covers turns,
intersections and four camera bearings across maps.

In the first confirmed control, `l2z2.p3d` dispatched at route time 824 ms and
completed at 13,034 ms. The next road section was requested at 8,641 ms while
that load remained active. Ground meshes were inserted and dispatched
progressively; section completion alone does not describe their availability.
For example, `trackShape_010` became resident at 10,492 ms and first dispatched
at 10,542 ms. Independent ground intersections remained available throughout
that first control even while visible scenery was late.

The worker profiler measured about 1,405 ms of active intervals for that file,
excluding explicit yielded intervals, against 12,210 ms elapsed. Chunk work and
native-bundle seeks dominated those measured intervals; PNG decoding contributed
about 18 ms. These are wall-time ownership samples, not pure CPU time.

The game loop sleeps to enforce its frame-rate limit while retaining the loader
mutex. That prevents pending loading from using the existing idle interval.
The private `bench_idle_loader=1` candidate lends the mutex during that wait,
using the existing GPU upload guard and loader reclaim mechanism. It does not
add a loading wait, change geometry or texture content, or increase population
and draw distances.

The first same-executable comparison moved `trackShape_010` residency from
10,492 ms to 7,180 ms and first dispatch from 10,542 ms to 7,215 ms. The next
road section completed at 12,159 ms instead of 22,227 ms. Measured position and
camera coverage matched in both directions. Instrumented loop means were
27.092 and 27.076 FPS, with p99 periods of 56.13 and 60.27 ms. These initial
results support the scheduling mechanism; they do not establish final visual
or performance acceptance. The change remains diagnostic-only pending repeat
captures, ordinary-driving comparisons and broader map coverage.

`RenderManager` processes zone transitions and missing files serially. It applies
the locator's dump operations before requesting incoming files. Later transitions
queue while a dynamic load is active. This makes request timing, queue residence,
resource dependencies and service time important candidates for investigation.

Road and zone files contain cross-file texture references. Reordering files by
their `r` or `z` filename prefix requires a dependency audit first.

## Passive incident recorder

The private `bench_passive=1` mode follows ordinary gameplay. It bypasses the
automation configuration, route allocation, forced level selection, vehicle
placement, steering, fixed seed and replay timestep. Controller inputs, random
seed selection and elapsed time pass through the normal game paths.

The existing diagnostic save-fixture mechanism makes private copies of
`Save1` through `Save4`. Gameplay reads and writes those copies. The owner's
original saves remain protected.

The player can use normal menus and drive normally in Level 2 or 3. After seeing
the transparent-ground failure, pressing **START + SELECT** requests an incident
report and returns to the development receiver. The recorder retains a bounded
rolling history targeting the preceding 20 seconds, plus a display capture taken
at the marker. Normal game exit also saves the trace.

The recorder distinguishes:

- Authored trigger entry and actual request enqueue.
- Dispatch to the Pure3D loader and its reported callback status.
- Section reservation, geometry insertion, section completion and unloading.
- Expected static geometry, current residency and draw dispatch during its display.
- The renderer's own sphere-culling decision, including distance and small-object limits.
- Occlusion-volume entry and exit, camera state and nearby scenery around the player.
- Independent resident-ground intersection queries, queue slots, sampled memory,
  pedestrian population and frame periods.
- Existing bounded worker timing samples for asset and texture-processing stages.

Continued driving does not prove that fresh collision data exists. Vehicle physics
can reuse the last cached ground plane when a terrain query fails. The recorder
therefore performs an independent ground query every 250 ms.

The current SGP2 catalog contains 5,166 static mesh probes across all seven maps,
extracted from the accepted enhanced assets. Release builds strip object names;
probe identity consequently uses native asset UIDs and exact bounding-sphere
bytes. Catalog collisions or malformed input are rejected. Instanced, animated
and dynamic geometry are outside this initial probe catalog.

The recorder samples spatial state at four times per second. Geometry insertion
and first draw-dispatch timestamps use the current frame clock. Normal-heap
sampling runs once per second; linear free memory is also recorded with frames.
These are sampled observations, not a guarantee that every transient allocation
peak was observed. Fixed startup memory preflight is separate from runtime memory
validation.

Passive history now uses the last gameplay timestamp and stores gameplay frames
only, so pausing before the marker cannot displace the preceding driving
history. The separate SELECT pause-dump shortcut is bypassed in passive mode.
Wide-mode display captures sample alternate source columns into a bounded
400-column image, preserving the complete field of view. Copy duration and
import, layout, cache and buffer-change failures are reported. The second
physical reproduction capture retained eight valid images with zero failures.

A Pure3D callback, a culling decision or a draw-dispatch counter does not prove
visible road pixels. Display captures use GSP display-capture information, validate
the buffer pointer before and after copying, and preserve the capture timestamp.
Exact association with the current CPU frame is deliberately not claimed.
Occlusion and additional rendering passes still require interpretation alongside
the actual image and the player's report.

Worker samples exclude explicit yielded intervals but remain wall-time samples,
not pure CPU-time measurements. Missing timing coverage is not zero cost.
This instrumented recorder is for diagnosis; its frame times are not an
uninstrumented performance comparison.

## Validation and acceptance

The recorder has passed ARM compilation, fixed 64 MiB startup preflight and host
checks covering original input/seed/timing pass-through, private-save isolation,
release-safe probe identity, draw generations, unload/reset and rolling-buffer
bounds. Those checks do not constitute physical incident validation.

Earlier exploratory drives are excluded from scenery and improvement claims.
One required recovery manoeuvres. Another used an observer that failed to match
release-stripped names and exhausted its initial observation buffer. The capture
API produced valid physical display images, but the observer's inferred missing
scenery was invalid. Native UID matching and bounded rolling storage address those
instrumentation defects.

A diagnostic-only predictive candidate exists behind
`bench_predictive_streaming=1`. It permits one additional exterior section under
slot and memory admission checks and leaves locator dump operations at their
normal trigger. It remains disabled and has not been accepted on hardware.
Population trials likewise remain private; the normal ambient limit stays four.

The next required evidence is an ordinary-play incident with valid probe binding,
known recording coverage and a useful marker image or player observation. Once
the failing stage is identified, test a focused fix against the same approach,
vehicle, speed profile, camera behaviour and asset-cache conditions. Broader
acceptance then requires representative physical traversal across additional maps,
including rapid direction changes and surrounding scenery, plus ordinary gameplay
confirmation. Frame-time, memory and population comparisons accompany that visual
evidence.

Private executables, source snapshots, checksums, receiver manifests and raw
captures remain under `out/local-dev/world-streaming-20261029/`. The lightweight
local executable mounts the exact accepted RomFS from its already verified,
read-only development asset container. Installed titles and original game data
are not deployment targets.

## Subsequent controlled experiments

On the extended recorded northbound route with modeled 12 ms main-thread work,
queue priority moved `l2r2.p3d` from behind eight character model/callback pairs to
immediate dispatch. It did not remove the preceding zone load: `l2z2.p3d` still
completed around 16 seconds into the traversal. The neighboring scenery remained
late. This isolates a real queue problem without establishing a complete fix.

Unbuffered native texture I/O did not improve this traversal. It moved about
446 ms of measured seek intervals into body-read intervals; the zone still took
about 15 seconds after dispatch. The original buffering remains the reference.
Nearby prefetch likewise left gaps on this short, cold approach. A warm-origin
revisit experiment reproduced the recorded unload followed by reentry. Its
retention candidate was rejected by the existing memory admission check, so that
run does not demonstrate a retention benefit. These are diagnostic focus sweeps,
not gameplay frame-rate acceptance.

## Raw I/O separation experiment

The normal Pure3D reader already has two 192 KiB buffers and uses the existing
asynchronous RAD file-I/O thread. Its construction thread cooperatively yields
while awaiting those reads. The prepared native texture bundle instead uses
synchronous stdio seeks and reads from inside the serialized loader path.

A private `bench_native_async=1` candidate routes those native bundle ranges
through the existing RAD I/O thread. One owned 32 KiB buffer holds raw bytes.
There is one outstanding range per stream and no additional reader thread.
The loader uses its existing mutex handoff while waiting. Header/source identity,
body hashing, construction, texture descriptor updates and publication remain in
the protected loader path. A failed read or validation cannot publish a native
texture; the existing source-image fallback remains available.

The reference and candidate must use the same executable, route, camera, modeled
load and accepted assets. `NATIVE_RAW_READER` reports range bytes, handoffs,
displayed frames while reads are pending, errors and elapsed read-wait time.
Neither a callback nor frame progress proves an improvement in visible geometry.
The physical comparison must also inspect residency, actual display captures,
frame periods and memory. Host checks cover bounded ranges, balanced handles,
main-thread fallback, corruption and read errors, plus the existing payload and
factory regression checks. Hardware acceptance is still pending.

The owner's proposed staged-reader approach is consistent with the separation of
raw storage and integration in [Unity's async upload pipeline](https://unity.com/blog/engine-platform/understanding-the-async-upload-pipeline).
[Epic's streaming settings](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/streaming-settings-of-the-unreal-engine-project-settings?lang=en-US)
likewise distinguish loading and registration budgets and granularity. Current
upstream [libctru RomFS](https://github.com/devkitPro/libctru/blob/master/libctru/source/romfs_dev.c)
uses offset-based `FSFILE_Read`; its file cursor seek updates the logical position.
Those sources explain possible mechanisms, not a measured result on this port.

The first physical raw-reader comparison activated the path: 239 ranges,
1,375,236 raw bytes, 132 presentations while ranges were pending, zero I/O errors
and the same 227 native hits/1,350,720 payload bytes as the reference. However,
`l2z2.p3d` completed at 19,060 ms versus 16,133 ms. Lower instrumented frame work
was accompanied by worse world latency. This candidate is not an accepted fix.
Its pending-I/O polling made 810,162 handoffs over the session. The next candidate
uses RAD file completion events, signaled directly by the existing drive thread.
The loader releases its engine mutex, sleeps on that event and reacquires the
mutex before protected work resumes. Host checks include completion before the
wait, balanced handles and at most one wait per pending operation. Physical
comparison of that revision is still required.

The event-based revision also completed the zone later: 19,096 ms, with 466 waits,
137 presentations while reads were pending and zero I/O errors. Both raw-reader
revisions remain disabled. These results reject per-range, on-demand staging as
an improvement on this route. They do not test genuinely ahead-of-demand,
coalesced reads. Small reads can each incur another cooperative loading turn.

## Asset ordering and displayed-ground evidence

The remaining ordinary-play incident was captured in Level 2. At the marker,
`l2r2.p3d` was still loading. Its request had waited 3,559 ms before dispatch;
the preceding `l2z2.p3d` load took 11,274 ms. The car's nearby collision query
still found ground, while the left-side display showed missing scenery. A
collision hit therefore cannot stand in for surrounding visual completeness.

Road geometry also appears late within the original world files. In `l2z2.p3d`,
two relevant road meshes begin at approximately 893,186 and 1,154,000 input bytes.
The private preparation tool moves self-contained collision chunks and
dependency-checked opaque road chunks to the front. Required local textures and
shaders precede those road meshes. Every original chunk remains byte-identical,
file size stays unchanged, and the remaining chunks retain their relative order.
Unknown, ambiguous or translucent dependencies cause rejection.

The private fixture selector uses original and reordered copies on the same SD
filesystem. The accepted RomFS and save files remain unchanged. This comparison
therefore does not confuse chunk order with RomFS-versus-SD storage behavior.

On the physical Old 3DS diagnostic traversal, road-first ordering moved two
observed road-mesh arrivals from 11,079/12,364 ms to 2,245/2,342 ms. Whole-file
completion changed little. The later matched midpoint captures provide pixel
evidence: at route times 9,819 ms for the original and 9,818 ms for road-first,
the original exposes the backdrop through missing ground and road-first shows
road. Recorded positions differ by 0.102 m, with matching camera direction.
The complete traversals have more than 99% matched position/camera coverage.

These are focus sweeps with modeled 12 ms main-thread work. Framebuffer age is
unknown. They demonstrate a local ground-availability improvement, not normal
gameplay performance or a complete fix. Surrounding scenery remains incomplete;
the next section still cannot start until the active file finishes. A matched
normal-driving comparison with capture and scene auditing disabled is required
before judging frame-time cost. Ordinary gameplay and broader map coverage
remain acceptance requirements.

Raw evidence and the exact comparison are in
`out/local-dev/world-streaming-20261029/l2-order-mid-comparison.json`, with the
original image under `l2-order-mid-control-1/` and road-first image under
`l2-order-mid-1/`. The experiment is private and has not been adopted for release.

The first normal-driving pair passed the route/camera match check: original
25.19 versus road-first 25.20 CPU-loop FPS, with no recovery. Frame-period p95
was 50.12 versus 49.21 ms; p99 was 57.51 versus 59.07 ms. This establishes no
meaningful average FPS gain. The reverse pair fell below the predeclared 90%
route/camera coverage threshold and is excluded from causal performance claims.
The four runs and exclusion are recorded in `l2-order-driving-abba.json`.

## Pedestrian supply and model reuse

Ordinary-drive counters found both rejected path choices and population-cap
exits. Source inspection confirms that the capped active list includes pedestrians
waiting for their character model: `Pedestrian::Activate` reserves the slot, and
`Pedestrian::Update` waits for `IsModelLoaded` before `ActivateSelf` makes it active.
Increasing the cap alone therefore does not address every cause of empty streets.

A private, default-off `bench_pedestrian_reuse=1` candidate prefers an already
loaded model held by a free pedestrian slot while shared loading is pending.
It preserves the selected model when already ready, filters against the current
group and per-model count limits, and keeps the normal cold-load fallback. It
adds no random draw and raises neither the ambient cap nor spawn distances.

Its first matched normal-driving pair had 24.995 FPS in both variants. Average
active pedestrians changed from 1.380 to 1.587; average frustum membership changed
from 0.854 to 0.881. Only four selections reused another ready model. These small
single-pair results do not demonstrate a noticeably more populated world. Frame
period p95 worsened from 49.02 to 50.98 ms; p99 improved from 62.02 to 60.06 ms.
The candidate remains unaccepted. Frustum membership is not visible-pixel proof.

The new reserved-slot counter in that pair produced zero samples because its
guard ran after the capture flag cleared. Those zero values are invalid evidence
about pending pedestrians. The guard is corrected and host-tested in a separate
frozen build; physical validation of that counter is still pending. Existing
frame, active-pedestrian and reuse counts in the pair remain valid.
